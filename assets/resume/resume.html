Ongoing Research Projects

Under Review & Preprints
CADENCE: Sensor-Grounded World-Consistent Gaussian SLAM for Collaborative Mobile ReconstructionUnder review
3D Vision · Collaborative SLAM — under double-blind review; arXiv preprint forthcoming

Collaborative 3D Gaussian-splatting SLAM across smartphones in which only deterministic geometric and physical tests can accept a cross-device link and per-modality confidences gate what a peer may change, so a degraded device cannot corrupt the shared map; false links stay below 0.5% on eight held-out Matterport3D houses, and a native iPhone app maps 15 Hz LiDAR in real time (20.3 ms p95 pose time).

Less Than Meets the Eye: Action-Set Identifiability and Principled Abstention for 3D Physical GeometryUnder review
3D Vision · Identifiability — under double-blind review; arXiv preprint forthcoming

Intervene3D decides whether a bounded set of camera motions can tell a real scene apart from its mirror reflection or an on-screen image, abstaining when no motion separates the explanations; abstention lowers false physical certainty from 0.832 to 0.000 on a pixel-identical benchmark, and viewpoint selection lowers ambiguous-region error by 31.3% on real photographs.

On-Device Sleep Staging from Wrist Actigraphy and Heart-Rate Variability: A CNN-SE-BiLSTM Sequence Modeling ApproachManuscript complete
Abrar Samin, Himika Tasnim, and Aniqua Nusrat Zereen — preparing for journal submission

Leakage-controlled, subject-independent four-stage sleep staging from a wrist wearable: 77.2% accuracy and Cohen’s κ of 0.68 (+7.9 points over logistic regression), a 3.28× distilled model for on-device use, and total sleep time that agrees with polysomnography (r = 0.96).

Patient-Referenced Evaluation of Clinical Findings and Re-Identification in Report-Conditioned Synthetic Chest RadiographsManuscript complete
Medical Imaging · Generative AI — preparing for journal submission

A patient-referenced protocol for what diffusion-generated chest X-rays carry beyond their prompt: across three generators and three cohorts, renders show findings the prompt omits far less often than the patient’s own radiograph, and re-identification against prompt-matched distractors falls close to chance.
